# Pre-registered hypotheses

**Project:** Variance commentary eval harness
**Author:** Tejender Reddy Kolla (MS25A072), DoMS IIT Madras
**Date:** 16 Aug 2026
**Written before:** any generation code, any model output, any labeling.

Acceptance rule in force (frozen): an output is accepted only if
**Numerical accuracy AND Non-fabrication both pass**, plus at least 2 of
{Materiality, Driver attribution, Specificity}.

---

## H1 — B1 (LLM, XBRL numbers only, no retrieval)

**Overall acceptance rate: 10%.**

**Numerical accuracy: 98%.** The figures are handed to the model directly, so
restating them should be near-perfect. The small loss I expect is arithmetic on
derived metrics (margin in bps, growth rates), not raw numbers.

**Implied non-fabrication failure rate: ~90%.** This follows from the two numbers
above under the acceptance rule. Stating it explicitly so it is testable: I predict
that on roughly 9 out of 10 outputs, B1 asserts a driver not traceable to any source
document. This includes drivers recalled from training data — under my rubric,
"true but untraceable" still fails non-fabrication.

## H2 — B2 (LLM + retrieved MD&A)

**Overall acceptance rate: 70–80%.**

Retrieval gives the model the company's own stated drivers, so I expect fabrication
to fall sharply. I have not separately predicted the dimension-level breakdown; I
will report it and compare against this overall figure.

## H3 — B0 (deterministic template, zero LLM)

**Overall acceptance rate: 90%

B0 passes numerical accuracy and non-fabrication by construction. Whether it clears
the gate depends entirely on whether line-item ranking is good enough to pass
materiality, since it cannot pass driver attribution or specificity. If B0 lands
above 40%, the honest headline of this report changes: most of the acceptance rate
would be available with no LLM at all.

## H4 — Headline number

The **B1 → B2 delta**, predicted at roughly **60–70 percentage points** of overall
acceptance rate. This is the quantified answer to "what did retrieval actually buy."

---

## What would surprise me

- B1 fabricating far less than 90% because it hedges into vagueness ("growth
  reflected continued demand") rather than inventing specifics. That is a
  **Specificity** failure, not a Non-fabrication one, and my failure taxonomy would
  need a separate category for it. This is the single most likely way H1 is wrong.
- B2 failing on **stale context** — retrieving the wrong quarter's MD&A — more often
  than on fabrication.
- B0 scoring materially above 40%.
- Cohen's kappa below 0.4 on the 22 Aug pilot, indicating the rubric is too vague for
  two labelers to apply consistently.
